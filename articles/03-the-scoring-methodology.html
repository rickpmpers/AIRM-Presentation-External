03 · The scoring methodology

10 vectors, multi-framework governance, actuarial loss modeling

The core approach is control-centric, quantifiable, and transparent: the system measures whether you've implemented and verified technical and governance defenses against specific, well-documented AI failure modes. From this deterministic foundation, an experimental actuarial engine models expected loss distributions and bridges technical evidence into underwriting language.

A five-level maturity scale

Every technical vector and governance control is evaluated on a standardized 0–4 maturity scale:

0No control: Missing entirely or undocumented.
1Ad-hoc: Informally implemented, inconsistent, or undocumented.
2Implemented: Defined, documented, and consistently functioning.
3Monitored: Implemented and continuously observed with real telemetry.
4Automated: Programmatically enforced, self-healing, or circuit-broken without human dependency.

Expanded governance-first standards catalog

AIRM maps control objectives against comprehensive international AI standards and frameworks:

  • NIST AI RMF 1.0 & Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1): GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE functions, including CBRN prevention, system prompt leakage, and embedding defenses.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 (AIMS): Formal AI management system policies, resource allocation, and organizational governance.
  • ISO/IEC 42005: AI system impact assessment methodology.
  • ISO/IEC 23894: AI risk management guidance and operational risk taxonomy.
  • OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025 Edition): Covers excessive agency, prompt injection, vector database weaknesses, unbounded consumption, and supply chain vulnerabilities.
  • MITRE ATLAS: Qualitative mapping to AI threat tactics and adversary techniques.
  • EU AI Act & NAIC Model Bulletin: High-risk classification controls and unfair discrimination governance.

Every tool output is normalized into an api.Evidence record with a canonical tool:// URI, runtime parameters, tool version, and execution timestamp.

The human-review invariant: An automated tool run generates diagnostic evidence with maturity explicitly left unassigned (nil). A tool run alone never grants a maturity score or establishes compliance; an authorized human reviewer must inspect the output and set the verified maturity.

Offensive-testing program schema

Adversarial evaluation is treated as formal governance evidence, not an ad-hoc penetration demo. AIRM enforces a validated three-phase lifecycle across ten attack families:

  1. TestGate: Formal pre-execution authorization, attack family classification, and scope boundaries.
  2. TestFinding: Empirical execution record documenting toolchain, payload, exploitability, and impact.
  3. TestDisposition: Post-execution remediation record, tracking re-test verification and sign-off.

Under schema validation rules, a test cannot be recorded as executed without a gate, and cannot be marked closed while an outstanding remediation retest remains unapproved.

Actuarial loss modeling & 75/25 AI-exclusion blend

To solve the insurance exclusion gap, AIRM provides an isolated actuarial engine in the pricing/ package that converts technical risk scores into financial loss language:

  • FAIR Monte Carlo Simulation: Uses compound-Poisson frequency (Loss Event Frequency) and lognormal severity (Loss Magnitude) to simulate annual loss distributions, calculating Expected Annual Loss (EAL), Value at Risk (VaR 95% and 99.5%), and Tail Value at Risk (TVaR 99.5%).
  • 75/25 AI-Exclusion Blend: Blends the AI-specific loss indication with underlying business class pure premiums using public benchmark observations: 
    Blended Pure Premium = 0.75 * AI Expected Annual Loss + 0.25 * Underlying Class Pure Premium
    followed by an explicit expense/profit loading factor.
  • Public Proxy Class Table: Calibrated against Coalition 2024 sector claim frequencies and severities across ten commercial sectors (healthcare, financial, retail, tech, etc.) with transparent non-ISO provenance disclosures.
