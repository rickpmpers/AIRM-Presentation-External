06 · Competitive landscape

What already exists, and where the gap is being closed

AIRM is not the first project to apply software and insurance thinking to AI risk. The competitive ecosystem includes specialist MGAs, governance platforms, carriers, and reinsurers. Understanding the field makes precise positioning credible rather than claiming to be the first at everything.

The market is already moving

The competitive field spans several distinct operating models:

  • Armilla AI: Specialist underwriting platform pairing algorithmic risk assessment with performance warranties and affirmative liability coverage.
  • Munich Re (aiSure) & Mosaic: Technical due diligence, model performance guarantees, and bespoke reinsurance backing.
  • Holistic AI: AI governance, compliance audits, and risk-quantification platforms mapping controls to the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and ISO 42001.
  • Traditional Cyber & Tech E&O: Treating AI exposure as an extension of existing cyber policies, often relying on high deductibles and broad exclusion riders.

These are not interchangeable approaches. Some sell assurance or performance guarantees, some provide governance workflows, and some provide underwriting capacity. The market is real, funded, and active.

Where current approaches fall short

  • Persistent agentic systems: Most platforms assess a single model endpoint; very few evaluate complex multi-agent swarms, autonomous tool execution, feedback loops, and cascading multi-provider failures.
  • Continuous runtime evidence vs. static questionnaires: Point-in-time assessments miss prompt drift, nondeterministic behavior, context degradation, and upstream vendor updates.
  • Verifiable local evidence boundaries: Enterprise engineering teams cannot export proprietary source code, internal logs, or cloud credentials to external SaaS auditors.
  • Translating technical signals into loss language: Point scores alone do not solve underwriting. Underwriters need expected annual loss (EAL), Value at Risk (VaR), and quantifiable rate adjustments.

AIRM's architectural answer

AIRM directly addresses these gaps through an integrated technical architecture:

AIRM combines:

  1. Multi-Format Architecture Intake: Ingesting 10 system specification formats to automatically detect AI components and specify telemetry needs.
  2. Cryptographic Dynamic Packaging: Delivering HMAC-SHA256 signed, environment-tailored telemetry packages from IC Cloud, verified bit-for-bit before local execution.
  3. In-Memory Credential Safety: Running collectors locally where cloud credentials never touch disk or egress to the network.
  4. Closed-Loop Telemetry Underwriting: Translating verified control screening into score-only exports that trigger dynamic carrier rate adjustments (e.g. -2.5% premium adjustment).
  5. Actuarial Loss Modeling: FAIR-style Monte Carlo loss simulation and 75/25 AI-exclusion gap pricing based on empirical claims proxies.
  6. Carrier White-Label Enablement (Decision 16): Allowing insurance carriers to distribute customer containers under their own branding.

What this means for the alpha/beta

We are actively engaging individual peer builders who operate complex, persistent agent swarms (such as OpenClaw operator architectures). Their feedback under real operating constraints verifies the local telemetry boundary, catches edge-case failures, and ensures the scoring model reflects actual technical resilience.
